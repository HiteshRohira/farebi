Same Same — Farebi’s impostor game

A rules-first product and implementation plan for a social deduction game where everyone receives a secret word, one player receives a near-match, and the room must identify who does not quite belong.

Product call

Recommended launch concept: Same Same

Everyone gets a word. Someone got the wrong one. Most players see the same secret; one player sees a related decoy. Nobody is told which side they are on. Each person locks a short clue, the clues appear together, the room debates, and everyone votes for the suspected impostor.

Why this fits Farebi: it reuses the platform’s strongest primitives—private information, simultaneous submissions, discussion, secret ballots, realtime phase changes, persistent room scores—and produces a different kind of bluff from Truth or Lie. The app supplies fairness and secrecy; the people supply the theatre.

Prompt quality is the game

Pairs must overlap enough to permit a plausible clue but differ enough to create evidence. Avoid obscure trivia, strict gender or class assumptions, region-specific knowledge in the Mixed pack, and pairs where one term contains the other.

Player-facing rulebook

Setup and objective
  • Players: 4–20; 5–10 is the sweet spot.
  • Round length: about 3–5 minutes.
  • Crew objective: use the clues to identify the player with the different word.
  • Impostor objective: blend in and redirect suspicion—even if they do not yet realize they are the impostor.
  • Information: every player sees a private word and a shared category; nobody sees a role label in Near Match mode.
  1. 1

    Peek planned

    Privately reveal your word, then confirm that you are ready. Keep the screen and word to yourself.

    DefaultNo timer
  2. 2

    Clue planned

    Everyone simultaneously writes one clue, 2–36 characters. Submissions stay hidden until all active players lock in or 60 seconds expire.

    AdvantageNo copying later clues
  3. 3

    Reveal planned

    All clues appear at once with player names in a server-randomized order. Read them before anyone argues their case.

    Duration10-second beat
  4. 4

    Interrogate planned

    The room gets 90 seconds to question clues and defend choices. Words remain secret; players may discuss clues, not display their screens.

    Host+30 sec / end early
  5. 5

    Vote planned

    Each player secretly chooses one other player. Votes lock individually and totals remain hidden until everyone votes or 45 seconds expire.

    RuleNo self-vote
  6. 6

    Runoff planned

    If the highest vote is tied, only tied suspects remain eligible for one 20-second revote. A second tie means the impostor escapes.

    FrequencyOnly when needed
  7. 7

    Unmask planned

    Reveal the accused player, both words, every role, the ballot map, and score deltas. The host can replay or return to the shelf.

    PayoffStaged reveal
What makes a legal clue?
  • Use one short word or phrase that connects to your secret without making it obvious.
  • Do not use the secret itself, a translation, a spelling fragment, initials, letter counts, rhymes, or “sounds like” tricks.
  • Do not show your screen, search the web, use gestures to spell, or coordinate a private code.
  • Repeated clues are allowed but suspicious. The server rejects an exact normalized match to the player’s own word; the social group enforces intent.
  • Blank or timed-out clues appear as “No clue,” which remains voteable evidence.
Rule-copy version

Give a clue that proves you know the word without giving the word away. One player has a different word. Find the clue that does not quite fit.

Experience and art direction

  • Shelf card: Add game 03 in src/routes/index.tsx with the line “Everyone gets a word. Someone got the wrong one.” Use electric violet (#B98CFF) as the contained accent against the current near-black/lime/coral system; keep the no-gradient rule.
  • Private reveal: A hold-to-reveal word card that snaps covered again on release. Category stays visible; the word uses oversized Farebi display type.
  • Clue wall: Named clue tiles land together after a short countdown. Random visual rotation can add party energy, but DOM order and focus order must stay stable.
  • Voting: Reuse recognizable player rows, but make suspect selection unmistakable and never expose live vote totals.
  • Reveal: Stage it in three beats: accused player, role, then the word pair and ballot map. Respect reduced-motion and provide an immediate Skip reveal control.
  • Mobile: Keep the primary action thumb-reachable, cover private content when the tab loses visibility where practical, and never rely on hover or color alone.
Visual-system fact versus older documentation

PROJECT.md describes monochrome/no-color UI, while the current routes already use lime, coral, cyan, and yellow game accents. Follow the implementation that users see: permanent dark foundations, one restrained accent per game, no gradients inside the game UI, and high-contrast shadcn controls.

Privacy contract

The prompt catalogue and selected pair remain server-side. getRoom returns a personalized privateBrief only for the current player during clue entry. Near Match must not return role labels before results. Never serialize the crew word, decoy word, role list, aggregate votes, or another player’s private brief into a pre-result client payload.

Mutations and pure game logic
  • startGame: accept impostor as a gameType, validate four players, choose a recent-safe pair, assign exactly one role server-side, randomize clue order, and enter impostor_clue.
  • submitImpostorClue: normalize whitespace, enforce 2–36 characters, reject the player’s exact word/aliases, write once, and atomically advance when the final clue lands.
  • submitImpostorVote: validate active player, stage, eligible target, no self-vote, and one ballot per player per stage.
  • advancePhase: extend timeout handling for clue, discussion, vote, and runoff; every transition re-checks state so competing clients are idempotent.
  • restartRound: clear round-specific prompt, clues, roles, and ballots while preserving scores and host settings; avoid the last five prompt IDs when the catalogue permits.
  • convex/lib/impostor-game.ts: own deterministic role assignment, prompt choice, clue normalization, tally/runoff resolution, and score calculation as pure tested functions.

Edge cases and guardrails

Risks that need explicit behavior
CaseSeverityRule / mitigation
Secret leakage through getRoomcriticalTest response shapes per player and per phase; return only privateBrief to the authenticated player.
Last clue submission races timerhighMutation re-reads status/deadline and transitions once; timeout fills missing clues with a neutral placeholder.
Player disconnectsmediumDeadlines guarantee progress; their clue may become No clue and their missing ballot counts as fooled for impostor scoring.
Player joins mid-roundmediumKeep the existing joinedForNextRound spectator behavior and never send current secrets.
Host leaveshighExisting platform has no visible host-transfer path. Treat this as a cross-game dependency; timers still progress when another client calls advancePhase.
Bad or offensive cluemediumV1 relies on private-room social moderation; add host hide/remove only if playtests show need. Curated prompts must remain family-safe.
Ambiguous prompt pairhighGive every pair a stable ID and log aggregate conviction rate; retire pairs far outside the target balance band.
Target balance band

Across enough plays, crew conviction should land around 45–65%. Above that, the pair is too obvious; below it, the pair is too similar or culturally unclear. This is a tuning heuristic, not a launch blocker before real play data exists.

Verification and acceptance

Automated checks
  • Role assignment produces exactly one impostor for every allowed player count and never runs client-side.
  • Prompt selection returns a valid pair, honors pack/difficulty, and avoids recent IDs when possible.
  • Clue normalization rejects whitespace-only, own word/alias, oversized, and repeat submission cases without leaking the other word.
  • Vote logic rejects self, duplicate, stale-stage, spectator, and invalid-target ballots.
  • Unique winner, wrong conviction, first tie, second tie, partial timeout, and score deltas all have unit coverage.
  • Existing Truth or Lie and Who’s That? tests remain unchanged and green.
Manual multiplayer matrix
  • Four players complete a normal round; only each player’s own word is visible before results.
  • A tied first vote enters runoff with exactly the tied candidates; a second tie ends once.
  • One player disconnects during clue entry and another during voting; both phases still finish on deadline.
  • A late joiner waits without receiving either word, then becomes active after replay.
  • Refresh during every state restores the correct private/public surface and never resubmits an action.
  • Host adds 30 seconds and ends clue/discussion/voting early; non-host calls are rejected.
  • Replay preserves cumulative scores, rotates role/prompt, clears old ballots, and does not repeat a recent pair.
Definition of done

A 4–20 player room can choose Same Same, complete a secret and realtime round without refresh, resolve ties deterministically, see an explainable reveal and score, replay safely, and switch back to another game—with no pre-result secret or vote leakage.

Chaos shelf — follow-on variants

  • Blank Slate: the impostor sees only the category, knows their role, and gets one exact-word steal attempt if convicted.
  • Double Trouble: two impostors share the decoy for groups of 8+, but do not know each other. One conviction decides the round.
  • Silent Mode: no discussion at all—read the clue wall and vote. Designed for 90-second rounds.
  • Hot Seat: after clues reveal, each player gets one yes/no question from the room before voting.
  • Chain Reaction: three linked words circulate across the room; players must discover both the odd player and which neighboring word they received.
  • Secret Scene: replace single words with prompts such as “worst place for a first date” versus “worst place for a job interview,” then reveal anonymous answers before names.
Expansion rule

Do not ship a variant because it is easy to toggle. Add it only when it creates a visibly different social decision and can still be explained in one sentence.
